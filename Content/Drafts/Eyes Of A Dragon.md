*We're losing the light. We won't reach the summit before dark.*

Weltzer just shook his head and chuckled. "How long has it been now? He said aloud as if to himself, "Since the war ended?"

*5 months.*

"And yet you still forget that I don't mind the dark. I thought you prided yourself on being observant."

It was silent in his head for a moment as he walked; following what was left of an old path that wound around the side of this mountain.

*I guess dying can change a person...*

"Not as much as you might think, Kuno."

Weltzer decided to stop their trek up the mountain for a moment. The mountain was steep and the edge of the worn steps formed a sharp drop. Weltzer walked up to the edge and looked down, feeling the wind pushing up the side of the mountain and letting it blow his hood back. He stood and watched the shadows slowly crawl over the land below as the day's last hours faded into darkness.

*It's strange. How a sunset is so distinct from the dawn.*

Weltzer remained silent and sat down on the edge of a large step, his legs dangling over the edge and his arms out behind him, propping him up as he leaned back.

*In the morning and evening the sun is at the same height in the sky, she shadows are of equal length, and yet you can always tell the difference between a rising and setting sun. Not just by it's actual movement, but by something I have yet to understand.*

"I may not know why or how they can be distinguished, but I've come to see that it's significant. A sunset is more than something to look at, it's something you feel. A sunset represents the end of a journey. It's beautiful for a few moments while you take in everything you were able to accomplish, but in the end if you dwell on your sunset too long your world starts to darken."

This time it was Kuno who remained silent in Weltzer's mind.

"I lost myself there once, long before you met me. I refused to move on and I trapped myself in a world of perpetual night. Sometimes the dusk arrives too soon, before you feel that you have accomplished everything you wanted to do that day. What I didn't realize until now is that every time the sun sets, that same sun is rising somewhere just beyond the horizon. Somewhere out of sight but there none the less."

*Weltzer.*

"You were the first rays of my new sunrise. You were the one who made me see that I needed to move on, to let go of the past and write a new story or two. It's funny, the world seems to be random and unforgiving, but in hindsight it always seems to have guided us exactly where we were meant to go. Always giving us clues along the way. I never would have guessed when I pulled the shield from the webbing just how significant it's crest would be for me. My entire life and the lives of everyone around me might have been drastically different had Xion actually taken the shield."

*But she did not. You did.*

"And yet every sun eventually sets to rise again. I hope that the sword and shield work for Avon the same way they did for me."

Again, Kuno did not reply. Weltzer stayed there for several more minutes watching the last of the Sun's light dip below the horizon, slowly drawing the cloak of night over the world. They stayed there in silence looking out over the world below them until the stars in the night sky were bright enough to soften the darkness that surrounded them.

*You should rest, the mountain will still be here in the morning.*

Weltzer stood up from the cliff and adjusted his belt, preparing to continue walking.

*Please...*

Weltzer was about to take a step but he paused. "Maybe you're right. Maybe some sleep would do me some good."

--

When Weltzer slept he didn't dream. Not anymore. His resurrection and assimilation of Kuno's aura had changed him. Whatever facet of his mind that controlled his nightly visions had been replaced by Kuno herself. His dreamscape became a permanent place for Kuno to exist so long as Weltzer lived. Kuno thought it was a beautiful place. It often appeared as a thick forest but if you wandered out far enough you would see mountains in the distance and at their feet a valley where the trees thinned and the blue water of a lake shimmered softly. It was a perfect place for Kuno to wander and explore, because near as she could tell it was an infinite space. The only problem was how empty it was. Here Kuno was alone, just her and her thoughts. Kuno was used to being alone but knowing that there was no one to seek out if she decided she even wanted some human contact was unsettling. But that changed when Weltzer slept. When he slept, instead of dreaming, he visited Kuno in her forest and was able to walk with her among the trees.

Kuno listened to his thoughts become slow a quiet as he began to drift off. She could tell he had fallen asleep when the forest had become silent. She waited a moment or two before Weltzer stepped out from the shadow of the canopy, materializing as he entered the column of light Kuno stood in.

"That was fast, usually it takes you a lot longer to show up."

Weltzer simply looked away

"Does this mean you are becoming more comfortable here?"

Weltzer was quiet and she wasn't sure he would answer her, but after some hesitation he replied. "I'm going to miss the feeling of sleep more than anything."

"You know you actually are sleeping, don't you? This isn't much different than a dream."

"It's not the same. I never dreamed very much, but I enjoyed the feeling of time passing and not doing or thinking anything." Weltzer looked back at Kuno and then back to the ground. "But I'll get used to it. It's such a small sacrifice to try and fix what I've broken."

"You know, there's no reason you can't sleep here."

Weltzer looked up at Kuno confused. She smiled back at him and he crossed his arms, considering her. "You want me to sleep. While I'm dreaming? To dream that I am sleeping?"

Kuno laughed and said "It doesn't seem that odd to me, I know plenty of people who dreamt they were doing mundane things, this wouldn't be much different."

“Here, let me help.” Veil stooped down and reached out to light the fire three of his comrades had been trying unsuccessfully to start with their flint and steel. Lighting crackled softly in his outstretched hand, casting purple light onto their faces.
Before he could touch the wood the person nearest him grabbed his arm and stopped him. “No. We don't need help.”
“Come on, the light is nearly gone, starting this in the dark will-”
“I said, *we don’t need your help.”*
Veil stood up and looked them each in the eye and they stared back aggressively, as if he had wronged them somehow. As if they hadn't known each other for well over a year. Veil felt estranged and alone, but more than that he felt angry. He was angry that those he would have once called friends were beginning to abandon him. It was a feeling he was all too familiar with. He closed his fist and let his lighting grow, enough to watch their eyes widen slightly, before letting the magic go as the light of a torch lit up the scene.
“Is everything alright?” Urien asked, holding his torch up. The 5 of them stood there in silence for a moment, the fire still unlit and the sun slowly sinking below the hill to the west.
After a moment Veil mumbled “Of course, everything is fine” And looked away.
Urien could see the flint and steel still in the hands of his friend. He sighed and touched his torch to the wood. The kindling at the center caught and began to glow brighter as a gentle breeze breathed life into the embers before igniting the larger sticks and branches. “I want you three on first watch” Urien said, “don't want any kind of trouble catching us by surprise.” And with a stern look to the one with flint and steel he turned and walked off back to his own tent, torch held high. Veil reluctantly followed him, biting his tongue before he could say any of the things he was thinking. “Better to just leave it be” he thought.

When Veil caught up and fell into step beside him Urien said “They will get used to it. Give them some time.”
“I just don't understand why it changes anything!” Veil said, “whether or not I can work magic doesn't change who I am.” Veil looked away again and Urien paused a moment before answering.
“You're right. It doesn't change who you are. Not intrinsically and not immediately. But your ability to perform magic helps define your choices which shapes the person you choose to become.” Veil remained silent as they arrived at Urien’s own fire. Urien sat down near it and Veil chose to stand, his arms crossed, frustrated. “This doesn't change who you are, but I can see why they are afraid of what magic might turn you into.”
At that Veil shot Urien a glare.
“Don't misunderstand. I'm not saying i believe it will, but you and I both know that some of the cruelest things in this world are done by mages, and for not much better reasons than because their magic allows them to do so. I understand their concern.”
“This is ridiculous.” Veil said, exacerbated, “it's not like I'm the only magic user here! [Iay’s sister] can do *significantly* more dangerous magic than I can, why aren't they afraid of *her?”*
“My guess would be that that is the only version of her they have ever known. People are afraid of change, and you’ve only been growing since you started working with us. It's not you they fear, it's your potential. And fear makes people do things they regret.”
“Is that why you hide your own magic? Because you are afraid people won't accept you?”
This time it was Urien’s turn to be silent.
Veil sat down and looked his friend in the eye over the flames of their fire. “I don't see how that's any better Urien, it's not something I would be proud of.”
“There is a difference between hiding a part of yourself and not drawing attention to that part. Finding that balance is hard but I like to think I try my best. Think about it. Making a small breeze to help feed their fire is very different compared to the way you approached them. But both actions aim to make good use of magic as a tool, but you don't have to be bold and brash to so so.”
“You know he’ll never understand that. He's not built that way.” Said a new voice.
Veil whipped his head around to his left, almost falling off the stump he had claimed as his seat. “Damn Nyx, if you keep trying to scare me like that you’ll end up hurt!”
Urien chuckled at this and Nyx just rolled her eyes and crossed her feet as she leaned back on her palms, taking in the warmth from the fire. “What, by you? You couldn't even hit me in a fair fight, what makes you think you’d react any better to a surprise attack?”
“Do you see my point veil?” Urien said with a smile, “Maybe try and learn from Nyx instead of competing with her, she seems to have a certain amount of tact about how she uses her skills.”
“That still doesn't solve the real problem.” Veil muttered.
“Doesn't solve what problem? It doesn't stop the world from judging you?” Nyx asked incredulously, “What, you think the world wronged you? Listen, that's just the way the world is. It's hard, and it's mean. It deals you a hand and leave the rest up to you. In the grand scheme of things you are too insignificant to be owed anything by fate.”
Veil looked to Urien for support but he shook his head and said nothing.
“You want people to like you? Then you have to make them respect you. Look at [Iay’s sister], people respect her because she's *effective.* They *need* her. The benefits of her magic outweigh any other negative opinion of her, she earned that. Or even Urien. Do you think if anyone realized he had magical talent that he would deny it? Do you think they would change their opinions of him?”
“I suppose not…” Veil said quietly
“Of course not. Without him this company would be in disarray, especially with no one to reign in Mavericks like you, it would be chaos. They need him, magic or not. And they respect the choices he’s made. Finding out he’s had some simple magic this whole time doesn't change the record he has already set.”
